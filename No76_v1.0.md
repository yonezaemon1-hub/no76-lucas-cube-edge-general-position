## Abstract

For each integer n >= 3, the edge general position number of the Lucas cube Lambda_n is F_n + F_{n-2}, with F_0 = 0 and F_1 = 1. The proof combines an analytic lower construction, star-forest and projection lemmas, local capacity inequalities, finite integer certificates, and a recurrence induction. It does not claim uniqueness of a maximum set and does not depend on the main theorem of Research No.75. Required exact whole-graph bases are n = 3, 4, 6. The 9,908 capacity bases, including overlaps, remain in the frozen verification lineage. The n = 6 relaxation margin of -2 is handled by the exact optimum 11.

# Frozen v0.1: lower construction and star capacity

# No.76 candidate v0.1 — A lower bound and star-capacity upper bounds for edge general position in Lucas cubes

PRIVATE HOLD. Preliminary results, not a completed all-dimension classification. NOVELTY INCONCLUSIVE / REFERENCES PARTIAL. No independent audit, Windows test, or human peer review performed. No.75 is unchanged; Section 2 below is reproduced as a self-contained argument, so this result does not depend on acceptance of No.75's Fibonacci cube classification.

## 1. Model and lower bound

Let Lambda_n, n>=3, be the graph of length-n binary words with no cyclically adjacent ones, with adjacency by a single-coordinate flip. Let F_0=0, F_1=1 and F_{r+2}=F_{r+1}+F_r. The cardinality of a largest edge general position set (at most two selected edges on each geodesic) is gp_e. Graph distance equals Hamming distance: remove the ones absent from the target, then insert the missing target ones. Consequently the colors (changed coordinates) on a geodesic are distinct. The number of vertices is the Lucas number F_{n-1}+F_{n+1}.

Klavzar and Tan (2023), Proposition 4.1(ii) and Theorem 4.6, give the coordinate-class size F_{n-1} and lower bound 2F_{n-1} for n>=4. Their Proposition 4.7 establishes gp_e(Lambda_5)=7, showing the lower bound 6 need not be optimal. These results are prior work.

**Theorem A (analytic lower bound).** For every n>=4,

    gp_e(Lambda_n) >= K_n := F_n+F_{n-2} = F_{n-1}+2F_{n-2}.

For n>=5, K_n=2F_{n-1}+F_{n-4}>2F_{n-1}.

Proof. Choose three consecutive cyclic coordinates, say 0,1,2. Select every edge changing one of the three whose other two bits are both zero. Its projection is an arm of the 3-star centered at 000. If a geodesic contains three selected edges, it must change each of the three coordinates once and these are the only changes to its projection. But the resulting 3-step projected walk would use three distinct edges of a star while changing distinct coordinates, impossible: a simple path in a star has length at most two. For the middle coordinate, its two neighbors are fixed zero and the other n-3 coordinates form a free path, giving F_{n-1} edges. For either outer coordinate, the chosen three and one additional neighbor are fixed (before the flip) to zero, leaving a free path of n-4 coordinates, giving F_{n-2} edges each, including n=4. The sum is K_n. For n>=5 the Fibonacci recurrence gives the displayed strict improvement. For n=3 the same construction selects all three edges, and K_3=3. QED.

## 2. Two star lemmas (self-contained; same arguments as in No.75 v0.10)

**Lemma B.** In any isometric subgraph of a hypercube, an edge general position set S using at least three colors induces a star forest on its incident vertices (only selected edges are included).

Proof. A simple 3-edge path in S is forbidden if its colors are distinct. Otherwise its colors are i,j,i. The four vertices realize all four assignments on i,j and agree elsewhere. The oriented length-2 subpaths have all four vertices as possible ending vertices. Choose a selected edge f of a third color k. Choose such a subpath ending at z whose i,j bits agree with the fixed bits of f. Choose endpoint y of f with its k bit equal to that of z. A shortest connector z to y changes none of i,j,k. Concatenating the length-2 subpath, connector, and f gives a walk with distinct changed coordinates throughout, hence a geodesic containing three selected edges, contradiction. The selected graph is bipartite and has no simple path of length 3. Every connected nonempty component must then be a star: two vertices of degree at least 2 would produce adjacent vertices of degree at least 2 (along a shortest connecting path), and these produce a simple 3-edge path in a bipartite graph. QED.

**Lemma C.** Suppose S contains a star centered at v with arms of colors I, where |I|=d>=3. Every selected edge f of color k has fixed bits on I excluding k either all equal to v there, or all opposite to v there.

Proof. Otherwise choose i,j in I excluding k for which exactly one bit disagrees with v. Orient the path v^i--v--v^j so its ending vertex agrees with f on i,j. Choose the endpoint of f that agrees with this ending vertex on k. A shortest connector avoids i,j,k, and the concatenated walk has no repeated changed coordinate, giving a forbidden geodesic. QED.

## 3. Star-capacity upper bound

Fix any d>=3 arms as in Lemma C, and let b be the projection of v to I. Let the core C be all legal words with projection either b or its bitwise complement. For x in C let a(x) count legal edges at x changing a color in I. Each selected edge of color in I joins exactly one core vertex to a noncore vertex: it agrees on the other d-1 coordinates, and since d>=3 the two core projections differ in more than one coordinate. Each selected edge of color outside I has both endpoints in C, by Lemma C.

**Lemma D.** |S| <= sum_{x in C} max(1,a(x)).

Proof. Charge each selected I-colored edge to its unique core endpoint. By Lemma B each selected component is a star. Assign each remaining selected edge (both endpoints core) to one of its leaf endpoints in that star, with an arbitrary orientation for a one-edge component. These recipients are distinct and have no selected I-colored edge, because their degree in S is one. A core vertex receiving an I-colored edge receives at most a(x) charges; a recipient of a core-core edge receives one. QED.

## 4. Exact cyclic counting and finite reduction

List the chosen coordinates cyclically, at successive distances g_0,...,g_{d-1}>=1, with sum n. A core projection b is feasible as a center of a star with those arms exactly when for each g_j=1 both adjacent chosen bits b_j,b_{j+1} are zero (cyclic indexing). Necessity follows from legality of each flip; sufficiency follows by setting all unchosen bits zero.

For fixed endpoint bits a,c of a gap g, record p,q, the bits immediately inside the gap next to the endpoints (for g=1 use p=c,q=a; for g=2 p=q is the same vertex). Legal assignment counts are:

- g=1: one assignment (p,q)=(c,a) if ac=0; otherwise none.
- g=2: one assignment for each p=q in {0,1} with ap=pc=0.
- g>=3: F_{g-1-p-q} assignments for each p,q in {0,1} with ap=qc=0. This follows from the adjacency transfer matrix [[1,1],[1,0]] raised to power g-2; a zero entry means no assignments.

Selections in the distinct gaps are independent with the chosen bits fixed. At chosen coordinate j the flip is legal exactly when q_{j-1}=p_j=0. Thus, for a fixed projection b, the total capacity H(g,b)=sum_{x with projection b} max(1,a(x)) is computed by summing over all gap records the product of their counts multiplied by max(1, number of chosen coordinates with both neighbors zero). The total core capacity is H(g,b)+H(g,1-b).

For any one gap g_j>=5, H satisfies the Fibonacci recurrence as a function of that gap alone: its local factor is F_{g_j-1-p-q}, and both smaller gaps remain >=3, so the same p,q records and factors can be used, with zero counts included. K_n satisfies the same recurrence in that gap. Feasibility of b is unchanged when reducing a gap >=5 to g_j-1 or g_j-2. Therefore margin K_n-H(g,b)-H(g,1-b) obeys the same recurrence. Strong induction on sum g_j reduces margin nonnegativity to gaps in {1,2,3,4}. If one specified gap is 1, it stays 1 throughout reduction.

**Theorem E (computer-assisted conditional upper bound).** For n>=5, if a selected edge general position set contains a star with at least four arms, its cardinality is strictly less than K_n. If it contains a three-arm star whose colors include two cyclically adjacent coordinates, its cardinality is at most K_n.

Proof. For d=4, the verifier checks all 1831 feasible base tuples, with nonnegative margin and the sole zero tuple all gaps one and all center bits zero (n=4). For any reduced tuple with an originally non-base gap, both smaller sums remain >=6, so strictness for n>=5 follows by induction. For d=3 with one or more cyclic gaps equal to one, all 64 feasible base tuples have nonnegative margin. The verifier compares the gap formula against an independent literal enumeration of every legal word and counts actual legal flips, and checks center feasibility by actual flips. Lemma D and the recurrence complete the asserted bounds. QED.

## 5. Finite exact values

The verifier generates forbidden triples in two independent ways: (i) BFS shortest-path DAGs with propagation of single-edge and two-edge prefix subsets; (ii) the three possible middle-edge choices and coordinate monotonicity. Both generate identical forbidden triples. A triple belongs to a shortest path iff some shortest-path DAG contains its three edges in order; all possible initial vertices are used. The monotonicity predicate uses equality of the middle edge and last edge on the first color, equality of the first and middle edge on the last color, opposite first/last fixed bits on the middle color, and absence of repeated coordinate changes outside the three colors. These conditions suffice because oriented endpoints can be joined by shortest connectors in the isometric graph.

Given the forbidden triples, the exact search uses an initially complete pair-compatibility graph on candidate edges. Each included edge makes two remaining edges incompatible if the three form a forbidden triple. After including an edge, candidates must also be its neighbors in the current compatibility graph. This maintains the invariant that a new candidate has no forbidden triple with two already selected edges, and future pairs must satisfy all chosen-edge restrictions. A greedy proper coloring of the current graph gives an upper bound on clique size; sorting vertices by nondecreasing color and branching in reverse order gives a bound for the remaining prefix. If selected count plus color bound or candidate count cannot beat the known witness, the branch is safely pruned. Otherwise all include/exclude choices are explored (exclusions proceed by deletion from the prefix). This is an integer-only exhaustive proof of no larger set, not a numerical solver assertion.

The computed exact optima are n=3..9: 3,4,7,11,18,29,47, all equal to K_n. The n=5 result is already in the primary reference; no novelty claimed for it. Exact upper bounds for n>=10 are not established here. Verification of witness counts through 20 is only a consistency check on the analytic lower bound, not an upper-bound proof.

## 6. Remaining work and literature

For a set using <=2 colors, its size is <=2F_{n-1}, strictly below K_n for n>=5. For a star forest of maximum degree one, the matching bound |S|<=|V|/2<K_n applies since |V|=F_{n-1}+F_{n+1}<2(F_n+F_{n-2}). Theorem E covers maximum degree >=4, and a degree-3 star containing adjacent colors. The remaining cases are star forests of maximum degree 2 using at least 3 colors, or maximum degree 3 where every 3-arm star has pairwise nonadjacent colors. Therefore the conjecture gp_e(Lambda_n)=K_n for all n>=4 is NOT proved. n=10 exact search was attempted outside this verifier but did not finish within the short time limit; it is not an impossibility result. No.75's frozen manuscript remains unmodified and is not required by the verification.

# No.76 v0.2 — Local star-pair capacity bounds in Lucas cubes

PRIVATE HOLD / NOVELTY INCONCLUSIVE / REFERENCES PARTIAL. This is an additive research note, not an all-dimension classification. The exact frozen v0.1 package is included. No.75 is unchanged. All new general implications require independent mathematics audit; Windows has not been run.

## 1. Model and inheritance

Use the model and Fibonacci indexing in FROZEN_V01.zip, RESULTS.md. Write K_n=F_n+F_{n-2}. The v0.1 construction gives gp_e(Lambda_n)>=K_n for n>=4, and exact finite values through n=9 were established there. For n>=5 the previous conditional upper bounds cover sets using at most two colors, matchings, degree >=4, and three-arm stars with adjacent colors. We use the self-contained star-forest and projection lemmas B and C from v0.1, not No.75's main theorem. All following arguments are for sets S using at least three colors, so the selected graph is a star forest by Lemma B. A color means a flipped coordinate.

## 2. Two-arm projection and capacity

The proof of Lemma C in v0.1 works without change for d=2 provided the edge under consideration has color outside the chosen pair. Indeed, select the chosen two arms i,j of a star with center v. For an edge f of color k outside {i,j}, a disagreement with v on exactly one of i,j permits orienting the two-edge path through v to end with the same i,j bits as f. Choose the endpoint of f with matching k bit. A shortest connector changes none of these three coordinates, and the concatenation changes every coordinate at most once. This is a forbidden geodesic. Consequently all outside-pair edges lie in the core C consisting of the two complementary patterns of center bits on the pair. Edges in the pair have exactly one endpoint in C (one bit flips). The capacity charging argument of Lemma D therefore works for d=2 also: charge pair-colored edges to the unique core endpoint, and outside-pair edges to distinct leaves of their selected star components. A leaf recipient has no selected pair-colored edge. The bound is the sum over core vertices x of max(1,a(x)), where a counts actual legal flips in the pair.

The gap counting and recurrence in v0.1 also work for d=2 whenever n>=3 (the two cyclic intervals have disjoint interiors). The new verifier exhausts all feasible gap tuples in {1,2,3,4}^2 with n>=3 for which the pair is adjacent or the center bits differ. It compares each fixed-pattern formula against literal legal-word enumeration and actual flips. All 24 bases satisfy capacity <=K_n, minimum margin zero. When reducing a gap >=5, adjacency status and the center pattern are preserved and both smaller sums are >=4. The identical Fibonacci recurrence for target and capacity proves the conditional upper bound at every n>=3. A triple with fewer than three selected colors was already covered by v0.1 for n>=5.

**Theorem Q (conditional).** If S contains a two-arm star with adjacent colors or unequal center bits on the pair, then |S|<=K_n. In particular, to seek a counterexample at n>=5 it suffices to consider stars whose every pair of arms is nonadjacent and has equal center bits. This is a genuine restriction, not a claim that all remaining configurations exist.

## 3. Three-arm supplementary cases

Use the original d=3 core capacity of Lemma D. The new verifier tests all feasible base triples with mixed center projection (not constant) or with every gap at least 3, and compares against literal enumeration. All 205 bases satisfy the target with minimum margin 4. Gap reduction preserves these conditions: reducing g>=5 leaves g>=3, and bits remain unchanged. By the recurrence, the conditional upper bound holds for every such tuple.

**Theorem R (conditional).** A three-arm star with nonconstant center projection or with all cyclic gaps at least 3 forces |S|<K_n. Together with v0.1, if a counterexample has maximum degree 3, every three-arm star must have constant center projection and at least one gap equal to 2; no gap is 1. This is a necessary condition, not a classification of remaining optima.

## 4. Local capacity by two-arm color pairs

Assume maximum selected degree 2 and that Theorem Q does not apply. Each two-arm component supplies an unordered pair {i,j} with nonadjacent coordinates and equal center bits. Let P be any nonempty collection of such pairs, and I the union of their coordinates. The two-arm projection lemma gives the following constraints simultaneously:
- Every selected edge of color k outside I has both endpoints satisfying equality of bits on every pair in P. Call this the core condition.
- A selected edge of color k in I satisfies the equality for every pair in P not containing k.
These are only necessary conditions. Ignoring additional forbidden triples is safe for an upper bound.

Partition legal words into fibers by fixing all coordinates outside I. Within a fiber, vertices are legal assignments to I. The eligible local edges are exactly those passing the above necessary conditions. A selected local subgraph is a forest of stars of maximum degree 2. Let A be any such local subgraph, e(A) its edge count, and z(A) the number of core vertices of local degree zero.

### 4.1 Arbitrary number of pairs

Charge every selected edge outside I to a leaf of its selected component (arbitrary endpoint if a singleton edge). Each recipient is core and has local degree zero; recipients are distinct. Therefore, adding the local edge counts and these charges over fibers proves

    2|S| <= sum_fibers max_A [2 e(A)+2 z(A)].

All maxima are finite local optimizations. They are over relaxations, not assumptions about the existence of globally compatible edges.

### 4.2 All two-arm pairs specified

If P is the complete set of pairs occurring at degree-2 vertices of S, every exterior edge is isolated in the selected graph: otherwise its color would belong to a pair in P, contradicting its being outside I. Count its two endpoints; both are distinct core vertices of local degree zero. Also require that every local degree-2 vertex uses a pair in P, since that pair occurs in S. Then

    2|S| <= sum_fibers max_A [2 e(A)+ z(A)],

with this additional local restriction. No exterior edges are assumed to be actually available at the counted vertices, so the bound remains a relaxation.

## 5. Cyclic gap summation, recurrence and certificate

Write d=|I|, list I cyclically, and let its cyclic gaps g_j be positive with sum n. For each fixed outside configuration, record the bits p,q adjacent to the selected endpoints inside each gap. For g=1 there is no outside vertex; use sentinel p=q=0, and enforce the adjacency of the two selected bits separately. For g=2 the one outside bit gives records p=q=0 and p=q=1, each multiplicity one. For g>=3 the number of legal internal words with ends p,q is F_{g-1-p-q}, including zeros. Gaps are independent before assigning the selected bits. A selected coordinate is blocked from value 1 if one of its outside neighbors is 1. Gap 1 imposes a direct adjacency between selected bits. Thus the local graph, core and objective depend only on the blocked mask and the gap-1 adjacencies, not on other gap lengths. The total local capacity is obtained by summing over record combinations with product multiplicities.

For a gap g>=5, the records can be considered the same four possible bit pairs, with zero multiplicities allowed, in both smaller gaps g-1,g-2 (both >=3). No gap-1 adjacency changes. Hence the doubled capacity satisfies the Fibonacci recurrence in each such gap; 2K_n does too. Restricting pairs to nonadjacent coordinates is preserved because gaps are only reduced to lengths >=3. For n>=5 and d>=3, a nonbase gap has smaller total n-2>=5; for one nonadjacent pair d=2, the other gap is >=2 and n-2>=5. Strong induction therefore extends every checked base inequality to all n>=5 for these cases.

The verifier computes the local optima twice: (i) enumeration of eligible local edge subsets with exact degree and star checks; (ii) enumeration of one-edge and two-arm components directly from legal flips and exact maximum-weight vertex-disjoint packing DP. For (ii), start from all core vertices credited, then a component on vertex set B with e edges contributes 2e - factor*|B intersect core|, with factor 1 or 2 as appropriate. Nonpositive contributions may be omitted because unused vertices retain their initial credit and no component is obligatory. Independent gap word enumeration checks the multiplicities through g=12. Both methods agree on every local optimum used in the finite cases. All calculations are integers; no numerical solver is used.

### Theorem S (conditional upper bounds for maximum degree 2)

1. If precisely one pair occurs, use section 4.2 with d=2. All 8 feasible base cases with n>=5 have doubled margin >=2.
2. If precisely two distinct pairs occur, either they share a coordinate (d=3), or they are disjoint (d=4). Relabel the cyclic order of their coordinates; the shared-coordinate case is covered by placing the shared coordinate first and using pairs (0,1),(0,2). For disjoint pairs there are exactly three pairings of four cyclically ordered coordinates, all tested separately. The complete-pair-set bound of section 4.2 passes all 36, 144, 255, 144 base cases respectively, doubled margins at least 2, 2, 1, 2 respectively.
3. If two pairs cross in cyclic order (four distinct coordinates i,j,k,l in order, with pairs {i,k},{j,l}), use section 4.1; no assumption on other pairs. All 255 bases have nonnegative doubled margin, minimum zero.

Each of these gives |S|<=K_n for all n>=5 by the preceding recurrence. Base enumeration excludes n<5 only, since the induction for nonbase tuples never enters n<5. This is a computer-assisted conditional upper theorem, not a solution of the unrestricted problem.

# No.76 v0.3 — Exact edge general position number of Lucas cubes (complete proof candidate)

PRIVATE HOLD / NOVELTY INCONCLUSIVE / REFERENCES PARTIAL. Computer-assisted proof candidate, not independently audited or peer reviewed. No Windows run. No.75 unchanged. The frozen v0.2 archive (and its nested v0.1) provide the earlier self-contained lemmas, lower construction, finite exact values, and conditional upper bounds. **Theorem (candidate).** For every n>=3, gp_e(Lambda_n)=K_n=F_n+F_{n-2}, with F_0=0,F_1=1. Model exactly as in frozen v0.1. Its Theorem A supplies the lower construction for n>=4 and the three-edge star for n=3. Its finite integer verifier covers n=3..9. It suffices to prove the upper bound for n>=5; v0.1 Theorem E and its discussion, and v0.2 Theorems Q,R,S, are used below.

## 1. Local upper bound for arbitrary maximum selected degree D=2 or 3

Consider an edge general position set S using at least three colors. By frozen Lemma B, S induces a star forest. By Theorem Q, we may assume all pairs of arms at selected centers are nonadjacent coordinates with equal center bits; otherwise the desired bound already holds. Let P be a nonempty subset of the distinct unordered arm pairs that occur anywhere in S (including the three pairs from a three-arm star), and I their union. By v0.2 Section 2, each edge of color k must have equal fixed bits on every pair in P not containing k. In particular, exterior edges (k outside I) have both endpoints in the core C of words satisfying all pair equalities. Freeze coordinates outside I to form fibers. Local eligible edges satisfy the indicated constraints for their color, and local subgraphs A of S are star forests of maximum degree D. Let e(A) count their selected edges, z(A) count the legal core vertices not incident to any local selected edge.

For arbitrary P, charge each exterior selected edge to a leaf endpoint of its original selected star, with an arbitrary endpoint for a singleton. These recipients are distinct core vertices, each with local degree zero. Consequently 2|S| <= sum_fibers max_A(2e(A)+2z(A)). This upper bound is valid for both D=2 and D=3, including when exterior edges share a center with local edges.

If P equals the complete set of all occurring arm pairs, every exterior selected edge is isolated (otherwise its color belongs to P). Its two distinct endpoints are core vertices of local degree zero. Every local degree>=2 vertex has every unordered pair of its incident colors in P. With this additional local constraint the sharper bound is 2|S| <= sum_fibers max_A(2e(A)+z(A)).

For D=2 and three disjoint pairs P, a simpler upper bound is useful. Each eligible edge of a color in I changes exactly one of the pairs and has precisely one core endpoint: bits on the other two pairs agree, and the changed pair toggles between equal and unequal. Charge each such selected edge to its unique core endpoint (at most two charges there), and charge exterior edges as above to distinct core vertices with local degree zero. Thus 2|S| <= sum_fibers 4|C_fiber|. This avoids all large local packing computations.

## 2. Recurrence and exact finite verification

List the d=|I| coordinates in cyclic order and g_j>=1 their cyclic gaps, sum n. Use v0.2 Section 5 gap counts: for g=1 a sentinel 00 with multiplicity 1 and a direct adjacency constraint; g=2 gives 00,11 each once; g>=3 gives F_{g-1-p-q} for p,q in {0,1}. The gap records determine the blocked mask and adjacency constraints on the selected coordinates; local eligible graphs/core and upper capacities depend only on these data and P. The total doubled upper bound B(g) is the sum of local values times product gap multiplicities. For any gap g_j>=5 the same four records can be used at g_j,g_j-1,g_j-2 (zero factors allowed); adjacency and nonadjacency of pairs are preserved, and B(g)=B(g-e_j)+B(g-2e_j). So M(g)=2K_n-B(g) satisfies the same recurrence in each large gap. In our sweeps d>=3, hence n-2>=5 if a gap is >=5. Induction reduces to gap tuples in {1,2,3,4} with n>=5 and nonadjacent pairs. This does not require all legal candidate tuples to occur in actual S.

The verifier independently computes local optimum for d<=5 by two methods. First, enumerate vertex sets of eligible one-, two-, or three-arm stars and solve exact disjoint vertex packing by subset DP; begin with all core vertices credited, each component contributes 2 times its edges less the core credit consumed. Omit nonpositive contributions since no component is mandatory. Second, branch on eligible edges independently, maintaining selected degrees and neighbor masks. Reject additions that exceed D, join vertices already of positive degree, or make a vertex of degree >=2 adjacent to another such vertex; these conditions exactly preserve a star forest because the eligible graph is simple bipartite. When P is complete, reject any new pair of incident colors outside P. The only bound used in this search is current incremental score plus twice the remaining edge count; selecting another edge can increase score by at most 2. Both methods agree at every local instance used. For d=6 (three disjoint pairs) use the preceding simple core bound, directly checking the unique-core-endpoint property. Gap masks are computed both from Fibonacci counts and by enumerating literal short gap words with OR convolution. No floating point or external solver.

## 3. Completion of maximum degree two

If there are at most two distinct arm pairs, or there is a crossing pair of pairs, frozen Theorem S applies. Otherwise choose any three distinct occurring pairs. They are noncrossing with four distinct alternating endpoints excluded, and all pairs nonadjacent and equal-bit at their centers. Their union has d between 3 and 6. Enumerate all three-element subsets of the unordered pairs of d labeled positions whose union is all d, reject crossing pairs, and quotient by cyclic rotations and reflection (preserving cyclic order or reversing it). This produces exactly eight representative patterns, listed by verify_v03.py; the rotation/reflection also permutes the gaps and legal assignments, so the canonical pattern suffices. Apply Section 1 arbitrary-P bound with D=2, using the simpler core bound at d=6. All 5,463 bases have M>=0. Section 2 proves the upper bound for every nonbase tuple as well.

## 4. Completion of maximum degree three

Select a three-arm star and let T be its three coordinates. By Theorem Q every pair in T has equal center bits and is nonadjacent. P_0 consists of the three pairs of T. If no other arm pair occurs anywhere in S, use Section 1 complete-P bound with D=3 and P=P_0. All 27 bases pass.

Otherwise choose any occurring pair p not in P_0 and use P=P_0 union {p}. Its union has d=4 or 5, since p has at least one coordinate outside T. Unlike Section 3 there is NO noncrossing restriction.
